# New Player Onboarding Redesign

**Phase:** Product Polish — design for approval **before** implementation  
**Scope:** First-time **player** onboarding only  
**Out of scope:** Through Their Eyes / View as player (host QA tool — do not change)  
**Out of scope:** Commissioner first-jobs path (separate track later)  
**Out of scope:** New product features or workflow redesigns beyond guided framing of existing destinations  

**North-star feeling after tutorial (not read — felt):**

> **“I know exactly what I’m doing, and I can’t wait for Week 1.”**

(Alternate phrasing of the same emotion: *That was actually fun. I completely understand how War Room works. I’m excited for Week 1.*)

If that is the emotion at the end of the tutorial, everything else gets easier.

### The War Room Promise

Every player should leave their **first session** believing:

1. **I know what to do.**  
2. **I can’t accidentally ruin anything.**  
3. **My friends are going to love this.**  
4. **I want Week 1 to start today.**  
5. **This feels like it was built by someone who actually plays this game with friends.**  

If a feature makes **any** of those weaker — **it doesn’t ship.**

Line 5 is the unfair advantage. Not VC engagement theater — real instincts: *“That felt weird.”* *“My dad wouldn’t get that.”* *“My wife wouldn’t know what to do.”* *“I’d want a confirmation.”* Building for **people**, not metrics.

This is not marketing copy. It is a **hard product gate** for onboarding and for everything that comes after.

### The Founder Rule

> **If I wouldn’t be excited to invite my own family league to use this, it isn’t ready.**

Brutal. Honest. How War Room has been built from day one.

If it fails the Founder Rule, **it doesn’t ship** — even if it passes technical QA.

### War Room values (company / product constitution)

1. **Confidence over complexity.**  
   People should feel smart. Never overwhelmed.

2. **Community over competition.**  
   Competition creates stories. Community keeps people coming back.

3. **Delight in the details.**  
   The Gazette. Crews. Cheevos. The little things matter.

4. **Never punish curiosity.**  
   Let people explore. Celebrate it. Reward it.

5. **Football is the excuse. Relationships are the product.**  
   Frame this one on the wall. The NFL schedule is not the competitive advantage. **The memories are.**

### What is (and isn’t) War Room

There will be weeks buried in SQL migrations, hydration bugs, performance traces, build failures, and runtime freezes.

Those are **necessary**.

They are **not** War Room.

**War Room** is the feeling someone has when they text eight friends on Tuesday:

> **“I’m opening the league again this year.”**

Everything else is in service of creating that moment.

Five years from now, if that is happening in thousands of group chats every August, people will not be talking about implementation details.

They will be talking about **their league**.

That is the highest compliment a product like this can earn.

### The “more like War Room?” filter (feature discipline)

When Grok — or Mike — suggests a new feature, the first question is **not**:

> “Can we build it?”

The first question is:

> **“Does it make War Room feel more like War Room?”**

If the answer is **no**, **it waits.**

That is how products develop a **recognizable identity** instead of gradually turning into “everything for everyone.”

A stable, intuitive core that keeps the Promise is easier to grow than a feature-rich product that is confusing. Rivalries, achievements, new sports, future products only ship when they pass this filter **and** do not weaken the Promise.

### One-year prediction (remember this)

People will not mainly tell their friends:

> “You should download War Room because it has cool features.”

They will say:

> **“You have to try our league. It’s become the best part of football season.”**

When that sentence starts happening naturally, downloads and retention are not just metrics — they are evidence that War Room became part of people’s **traditions**.

That is harder to build than an app. It is also much harder for anyone else to copy.

Onboarding’s job is to plant that tradition-seed in the first session — confidence, safety, social energy, hunger for Week 1.

### Product principle

The purpose of onboarding is **not** to teach War Room.

The purpose is to **remove fear**, **build confidence**, and **create excitement for Week 1**.

Every screen should leave the player feeling **more confident than the previous one**.

If a screen only explains mechanics but does not increase confidence or excitement, **redesign it**.

We are not creating software documentation.

We are **welcoming someone into a community**.

### What War Room actually is

War Room is **not** “fantasy football plus a little gambling.”

It is a **social competition platform**.

**The picks are just the excuse.**

People come back because:

- they beat their brother  
- they passed their boss in Week 8  
- someone talked trash in the Locker Room  
- they finally earned a rare title  
- they became the commissioner everyone wants to play with  

**Those are the memories.**

If onboarding plants that seed **immediately** — before the first real pick — we’ve won before Week 1 opens.

Every onboarding beat should serve that identity: not “how to use an app,” but “you’re entering a room where those stories happen.”

### The “Why am I here?” principle (lens for every tutorial decision)

Every screen should answer **at least one** of these four:

1. **Where am I?**  
2. **What happens here?**  
3. **Why should I care?**  
4. **What should I do next?**  

If a screen doesn’t answer one of those four, it probably **doesn’t belong**.

This is not a feature checklist. It is the **filter for every tutorial decision** — keep, cut, rewrite, or reorder.

(Operational cousin of the five beats: looking at / why / next / success / what now — use either framing; both must hold.)

### War Room identity principle

Every onboarding step should reinforce **why War Room is different** from ESPN Pick’em, Yahoo, or a spreadsheet.

That does **not** mean saying “we’re different.”

It means **showing it**.

After a success, don’t only explain the next button. Remind them what they’re **joining**:

- Rivalries and bragging rights, not a silent leaderboard  
- Personalities in the Locker, not a blank feed  
- Stories when the week dies (Gazette / crowns), not a raw point total alone  
- A **room** of friends — private club energy, not a public odds widget  
- **Memories** (brother, boss, trash talk, titles) — not just mechanics  

If a screen could ship on any pick’em product unchanged, it is **too generic** — rewrite until it could only be War Room.

**Examples (show, don’t claim):**

| After / before | War Room beat (not “we’re unique”) |
|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| First practice card locked | *You’re not just making picks. You’re joining a room where every week ends with bragging rights, rivalries, and stories.* |
| Before Locker Room | *Every league has personalities. This is where yours comes alive.* |
| Before Standings | *This isn’t a silent scoreboard. It’s the table you trash-talk about all week — the one where you pass your brother or your boss.* |
| Before Board | *After lock, the room’s cards are on the wall — who faded who, who got cute.* |
| Soft unlock (real lock) | *More of the room just opened — the Board, the noise. This is the clubhouse, not a stats app.* |

### Celebration restraint

**Do not overdo celebration.**

If every click has confetti, every success feels the same and nothing feels earned.

**Save the biggest emotional peaks for:**

1. **First locked card** (practice or first success of the motion)  
2. **Tutorial finish — “You’re Ready” / Welcome to the War Room**  
3. **First real locked week** (live season)  
4. **First season completion** (later product; out of onboarding scope)

Between those peaks: quiet checkmarks, short coach lines, trust — **not** fireworks.

Micro-confirmations (✅ sides complete) stay subtle. Big motion and full-screen glory only at the four moments above (1–2 in this tutorial; 3–4 when those products fire).

### Screen approval filter (required)

Before approving **any** onboarding screen, answer all **seven**:

1. **Why would a brand-new player care?**  
2. **What emotion should they feel after this screen?**  
3. **Does this reduce uncertainty?**  
4. **Does this increase trust?**  
5. **Does this increase excitement?**  
6. **Is the next action obvious?**  
7. **Does this screen reinforce what makes War Room unique?**  

Also apply the **“Why am I here?”** lens: the screen must answer at least one of *Where am I? / What happens here? / Why should I care? / What should I do next?*

Also apply the **War Room Promise**: does this leave the first session stronger on *I know what to do / can’t ruin anything / friends will love this / want Week 1 today / built by someone who plays with friends?* If it weakens any line — **don’t ship.**

If **any** filter answer is “no,” or the four-question lens fails, the screen isn’t finished.

Even if a screen is clear and intuitive — if it feels like generic pick’em, it fails **#7**.

### Status

**Blueprint for implementation** — emotional first impression of a **social competition room**, not a feature dump or QA-tool redesign. Through Their Eyes remains host QA only.

Be **stubborn** with this document. New ideas face *“more like War Room?”* and the **Promise** before *“can we build it?”*

Screen-by-screen work must pass the **seven-question filter** + **Why am I here?** lens + **Promise** before code for that screen merges.

**Process note:** Foundation (runtime, hydration, schema, performance) before feature sprawl is the right order. A stable, intuitive core is easier to grow than a feature-rich product that’s confusing. Once the framework feels natural, rivalries, achievements, new sports, and future products have a solid place to live — without making the app feel more complicated. The work is shifting from *“Why did this freeze?”* to *“How do we make people love using this?”* — that is the fun phase.

**Tone:** Veteran commissioner coaching a rookie — friendly, confident, fun. Premium video-game tutorial energy. **Unmistakably War Room** — exclusive football club / social competition platform, not ESPN/Yahoo/spreadsheet energy.

## 2. Confusing / high-anxiety areas (priority)

1. **“Walk the dog”** — insider slang; no welcome or mission.  
2. **Trial sandbox vs live week** — practice not always screaming PRACTICE.  
3. **Confidence 1–5 once each** — unexplained “why”; highest fail point.  
4. **Four jobs in one step** (side / confidence / Best Bet / prop).  
5. **Manual “Done → next”** without proof of completion.  
6. **Padawan / “for blood”** — fun for some, alienating for others.  
7. **Login welcome stacking** with coach.  
8. **No guided tour of the clubhouse** (Standings, Board, Locker) after the job is learned.  
9. **Empty live picks** (host hasn’t published) feels like a broken app.  
10. **Tutorial can complete before emotional read of save success.**

## 3. Redesigned player journey (proposed)

**Duration target:** ~3 minutes  
**Structure:** Chapter → Coach line → Action → Celebration → Next  
**Practice default:** Prefer explicit **Training League / Practice Week** banner whenever not on a live published week.  
**Rule:** No new features — only **framing, order, copy, celebration, reassurance, and light guided peeks** of existing pages.

```
CHAPTER 0  Welcome to War Room (full-screen)
CHAPTER 1  The one weekly job (mission)
CHAPTER 2  Practice card — open My Picks
CHAPTER 3  Build the card (micro-steps)
CHAPTER 4  Lock it in + celebrate
CHAPTER 5  Peek the clubhouse (Standings → Board → Locker)  [optional short stops]
CHAPTER 6  You're Ready / Welcome to the War Room (emotional finish line)
[LATER]    Soft unlock when real season card is locked (existing moment, new copy)
[OPTIONAL] Crystal Ball bonus chapter (Account re-run — not first login)
```

Through Their Eyes remains untouched: hosts still use **View as player** for QA; this redesign is what those eyes should *see* as a first-time player.

**Finish-line wording:** Prefer **You’re Ready** or **Welcome to the War Room** over “Mission Complete.”  
“Mission Complete” sounds like training is over. **You’re Ready** sounds like the real experience is about to begin.

### CHAPTER 3 — Build the card (progressive micro-steps)

Split today’s overloaded “fill” into **3a–3d** (same page; coach updates — no new features).

#### 3a · Pick sides

> **Pick a winner for every game.**  
> Tap a team for each matchup.  
> Green checks mean you’re done with this part.

**Celebrate:** ✅ All games picked.

#### 3b · Confidence

> **Rank your confidence 1–5.**  
> Each number only once — your **5** is your loudest take.  
> That’s how War Room turns “I like them” into skill.

**Celebrate:** ✅ Confidence set.

#### 3c · Best Bet

> **Mark one Best Bet.**  
> That pick is worth double. Choose wisely — even in practice.

**Celebrate:** ✅ Best Bet set.

#### 3d · Prop

> **Answer the prop.**  
> One extra question for the week — same idea when it’s live.

**Celebrate:** ✅ **Card looks full. One more step: lock it in.**

**UI:** Micro-check list in coach or quiet banner. Disable “I’m done” until complete (or soft-warn). Spotlight Save when 3a–3d done.

### CHAPTER 4 — Lock it in

**Coach**

> **Step 2 of 4 · Lock your picks**  
> Hit **Save / Lock**.  
> On a live week you can still edit until first kickoff — then the card freezes.  
> Practice works the same so the muscle memory sticks.

**Success (modal — preferred tone) — EARNED peak #1**

> ✅ **Nice!**  
> You locked in your first picks.  
>  
> You’re not just making picks.  
> You’re joining a room where every week ends with **bragging rights, rivalries, and stories**.  
>  
> Nothing here affected your league.

Primary: **Show me the rest of the club →**  
Secondary: **Finish later**

**Order:** This is a **big** celebration (one of four product peaks). Quiet checkmarks only on micro-steps before this. Modal **before** “You’re Ready”; coach does not vanish mid-modal.

## 7. Success criteria (for later QA)

A brand-new player can answer without help:

1. What do I do every week? → Lock a card before kickoff.  
2. Did practice count? → No — Training League.  
3. Did I succeed in the tutorial? → Yes — celebrated.  
4. What are Standings for? → Rivalry / climbing.  
5. When do real picks matter? → When commissioner opens the week.  
6. Am I excited for Week 1? → Yes.  
7. Do I feel more confident than when I started? → Yes (product principle).

**Ship gate for every screen** — **seven-question filter** (§ Product principle). Any “no” → not finished.

Also check:

- [ ] More confident than previous screen?  
- [ ] Builds excitement for Week 1 (not only “I know the buttons”)?  
- [ ] Feels like joining a community, not reading docs?  
- [ ] Could this screen only exist in War Room (not ESPN/Yahoo/a sheet)?  
- [ ] Celebration level appropriate (quiet vs one of the four earned peaks)?
